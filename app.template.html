How a prediction is made

Every team carries an Elo rating built from run margin, regressed toward the middle between seasons and adjusted for the quality of the schedule it has actually faced. Every starting pitcher carries his own Elo, updated after each start on the runs he allowed per out against the offence in front of him, at that park. Every hitter carries an Elo updated on wOBA per plate appearance against the pitcher he faced. Bullpens and team run prevention are rated the same way.

On top of the ratings the model uses starter rest, bullpen outs over the last three days, the platoon edges in the posted lineup, the hitters' own record against the man on the mound, that pitcher's record against this opponent, park run factor, altitude, temperature and the wind component blowing out to centre field, travel distance and time-zone shift, day or night, doubleheaders, series game number, head to head, last-10 and last-30 form, and how many regulars are missing from the card.

A logistic model fitted on past seasons turns all of that into a win probability for the home team. Its regularisation is chosen by out-of-fold log loss and its confidence is then rescaled by a temperature fitted on games it never trained on, because a raw model that says 80% tends to win nearer 72% — and that gap is exactly where a Kelly stake does the most damage. The result is blended with the market price using weights fitted on past games where closing prices were logged; until you log some, it falls back to a fixed 35/65 blend that leans on the market.

Lineups post two to four hours before first pitch. Until they do, the app shows the nine who most recently started in each order slot — so before lineups are out, treat every number as provisional. You can change any hitter or either starter and the probability is recomputed from the ratings, not looked up.
